# Sample data for design mocks

Realistic, **internally consistent** numbers for every screen. Use only these values in mocks: no lorem ipsum, no invented stats. All values are fictional (2026 Week 6), but the math holds together (edges, tiers, records and units agree with each other and with the conventions below).

## Conventions (how to read and display these numbers)
- **Spreads** are shown from the favorite's side: `OSU −27.5` means Ohio State is favored by 27.5. `PK` means pick'em (no favorite). Use a true minus sign (−), not a hyphen.
- **Model line** uses the same format: `OSU −31.2` means the model thinks Ohio State wins by 31.2.
- **Edge** is the gap between model and market in points, labeled with the side it favors: `+3.7 OSU` means the model likes Ohio State 3.7 points more than the market does. The edge side is *not* always the favorite. In Texas vs Oklahoma, Texas is favored but the edge is on Oklahoma.
- **Cover %** is the model's probability that the edge side covers. **Win %** is the model's straight-up win probability for the team it favors.
- **Tiers:** A = edge ≥ 5.0, B = ≥ 3.5, C = ≥ 2.5. Below 2.5 there's no pick (shown as —).
- **Ranks** next to team names are the **model's** power ranks, not AP. Show them for the top 25 only.
- **Ratings** are points better than an average FBS team on a neutral field. Home field is worth +2.3.
- **Units** assume −110 pricing: a win is +1.0u, a loss −1.1u. Break-even is 52.4%. In the pick'em contest there's no price, so break-even is 50%.
